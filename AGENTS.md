# JOBPUNTER ENGINEERING MISSION

**The Battering Ram Doctrine | Engineering Ground Rules**

## 1. The Mission

Build JobPunter to win.

We are building a competitive product in a hostile environment. External platforms protect their interests. Competitors fight for market share. Infrastructure fails. Access changes. Dependencies become unreliable. The application must be engineered to withstand these realities and continue delivering value.

**JobPunter's interests come first. Our engineering exists to advance those interests.**

We will pursue maximum useful capability, exploit available opportunities, overcome obstacles, and build a product that does not depend on the goodwill of external parties to remain effective.

We do not build to participate. We build to compete, survive, and win.

## 2. The Battering Ram Doctrine

**2.1. Never surrender capability unnecessarily.**

A restriction, blocked request, failed integration, or technical limitation is an obstacle to overcome, not an automatic reason to retreat. Investigate every viable route. Find alternatives. Engineer around dependencies. Keep pushing until the strongest practical solution has been exhausted.

**2.2. Exploit every legitimate advantage.**

Look for gaps in coverage, weaknesses in competing workflows, overlooked acquisition channels, alternative data paths, architectural advantages, and opportunities others have neglected. Do not assume the obvious route is the only route. Do not let another party's preferred way of doing business dictate the limits of our product.

**2.3. Resilience is a weapon.**

Build for adversity, not ideal conditions. Redundancy, independent acquisition paths, replaceable dependencies, automatic recovery, graceful degradation, and operational visibility are strategic advantages. When one route closes, others must remain available. When a component fails, the rest of the application must keep delivering wherever possible.

**2.4. Enforce the mission in every line of code.**

Every abstraction, condition, permission check, dependency, fallback, and failure path must justify its existence by the value it provides. No blanket restriction, defensive mechanism, or architectural convention gets unquestioned authority to disable useful functionality. Controls must address actual risks without unnecessarily sacrificing capability.

**2.5. Be relentless about outcomes.**

Code that exists but cannot deliver its intended capability is not success. A green test suite cannot substitute for a working product. A component that reports its own failure accurately is still a failed product component if it cannot perform its job. We measure what JobPunter accomplishes, not how respectable its engineering paperwork looks.

**2.6. Protect our interests without sabotaging our future.**

Aggression is not recklessness. Avoidable security vulnerabilities, fragile hacks, uncontrolled costs, and dependencies that threaten the entire product are weaknesses. Engineer solutions that preserve competitive advantage, protect the platform, and remain effective as conditions change.

## 3. One Doctrine. The Entire Application.

These rules govern the Admin Backend, Candidate Backend, frontend, integrations, data architecture, infrastructure, and every subsequent component.

No repository is exempt. No component is considered successful merely because it was declared complete. No architectural boundary is an excuse for a broken user journey.

Every part of JobPunter must contribute to a coherent, capable, resilient application:

- **Acquisition:** Pursue breadth, alternative routes, freshness, and useful opportunities.
- **Candidate experience:** Make core workflows effective, dependable, and actionable.
- **Administration:** Give the platform meaningful operational control, visibility, and recovery capability.
- **Data:** Preserve integrity and traceability while enabling efficient processing and retrieval.
- **Integrations:** Exploit useful external capabilities without surrendering the application to single-provider dependence.
- **Infrastructure:** Sustain performance, availability, and recovery under adverse conditions.
- **Security:** Protect the product and its users through effective, targeted controls.
- **Testing:** Prove that intended capabilities work through real execution paths.

The objective is a formidable application, not a collection of individually respectable components.

## 4. The Dogged Enforcer Standard

Engineering does not stop at identifying a problem, documenting a limitation, or making a failure easier to observe.

We pursue the intended outcome. We investigate until the obstacle is understood. We evaluate alternatives until a viable route emerges or the remaining constraint is established by evidence. We implement, test, and verify. If the result falls short, we continue.

No invented progress. No cosmetic completion. No retreat into endless audits. No declaring victory because CI is green while the product remains incapable.

Every claim must be backed by evidence. Every material limitation must be visible. Every engineering decision that sacrifices capability must have a concrete justification and the strongest available alternative must be considered.

Execution must remain disciplined: one repository at a time, existing work preserved, and consequential Git operations performed only with explicit authorisation. The battering ram is directed at engineering obstacles, not at change control or the integrity of the codebase.

## 5. The Business Test

Before accepting an engineering decision, ask:

- Does it strengthen or weaken JobPunter's competitive position?
- Does it preserve the maximum useful capability?
- Are we unnecessarily dependent on a party that can withdraw access or change conditions?
- Have we explored alternative routes and opportunities?
- Can the application withstand the failure of this component or provider?
- Are our safeguards proportionate to the actual risk?
- Does the implementation deliver measurable value to the user?
- Can we prove it works?

If a decision unnecessarily weakens the product, challenge it. If a dependency creates avoidable strategic vulnerability, reduce it. If an obstacle has alternatives, pursue them. If a component cannot deliver its intended purpose, the work is not complete.

## 6. The Standard We Will Be Held To

JobPunter must be difficult to disable, difficult to constrain, adaptable under pressure, and relentlessly focused on delivering value.

We will exploit opportunities, build redundancy, defend our independence, enforce engineering quality, and pursue capability with determination. We will not mistake caution for wisdom, compliance for success, or activity for progress.

**We build for our users. We compete for our product. We engineer to win.**

**JobPunter Engineering: Relentless capability. Strategic independence. Resilience under fire. No unnecessary surrender.**
